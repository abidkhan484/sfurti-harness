# Sfurti mission

This principle-based reference is distilled from the mission supplied by the user. It guides research, selection, creation, and review. Current audiences, channels, products, prices, and execution choices belong in the [harness design](harness-design.md), not here.

Sfurti helps children grow through meaningful, effortful experiences that cultivate curiosity, creativity, problem-solving, sustained engagement, and willingness to explore, practice, and improve. Keeping children busy or reducing screen use alone does not establish alignment.

## What the child does

The central distinction is passive consumption versus active, purposeful engagement. Ask what the child decides, creates, solves, practices, experiments with, or explores. Meaningful effort is the primary filter. Physical, hands-on experiences are strongly favored where they support tangible feedback, movement, manipulation, experimentation, and interaction with the real world.

Purposeful digital creation or practice can align with the mission. A physical activity can be passive. Evaluate participation rather than treating the presence of a screen as the deciding factor.

## Experience principles

- Curiosity → action → difficulty → discovery → competence is a lens, not a mandatory sequence.
- Effort includes making, building, imagining, reasoning, experimenting, practicing, manipulating materials, solving, developing skills, and sustained exploration.
- Difficulty should support engagement and growth; unnecessary frustration is not a benefit.
- Make room for structured mastery and open-ended creation.
- Favor experiences that are easy to enter, interesting to explore, and capable of becoming richer over time.
- Offer exploration, allow interest to emerge, and make deeper engagement available. Avoid endless switching and forced early specialization.
- Invite, enable, scaffold, and let the child act. Materials, guidance, and next steps should preserve meaningful choice.
- Start from genuine interest and expand gently. Adult goals can shape the environment without erasing the child's agency.

## Hard stops

Reject content or experiences that rely on shame, fear, coercion, or guilt directed at parents or children; forced participation presented as engagement; demonization of screens or technology; screen-free status as sufficient proof of value; difficulty added for its own sake; unsupported developmental, psychological, educational, or medical claims; or manipulative appeals to parental anxiety.

## Evaluation

Explain what the child actually does, where effort occurs, whether curiosity and agency remain, and whether engagement can deepen. Assess whether guidance scaffolds rather than forces, whether physical interaction helps, and what technology enables.

- **On-mission:** meaningful participation and effort, respecting agency and the hard stops.
- **Borderline:** useful elements with risks of passivity, shallow engagement, excessive direction, or rapid exhaustion; explain what design change would help.
- **Off-mission:** primarily captures attention without meaningful participation, removes useful agency, or violates a hard stop.

Parent-facing content should accurately invite or enable these experiences. Watching a Sfurti post is not itself evidence that a child has gained a developmental benefit.

## North-star test

Does this create a meaningful opportunity for a child to actively engage, put in effort, explore, create, solve, practice, or grow while preserving curiosity and agency?
